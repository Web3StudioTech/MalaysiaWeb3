DeFi Fundamentals

How DeFi works

Decentralised Finance replaces traditional financial intermediaries with smart contracts — self-executing code on blockchains that handle lending, trading, and yield generation automatically.

01
Liquidity Pools & AMMs
Instead of order books, AMMs (Automated Market Makers) use pools of two tokens. You deposit both sides to become a liquidity provider (LP) and earn trading fees. Risk: impermanent loss.
02
Lending & Borrowing
Deposit assets to earn interest from borrowers. Borrow against your collateral. Protocols like Aave and Compound manage this automatically. Risk: liquidation if collateral drops.
03
Yield Farming
Earn protocol tokens on top of base yields by staking LP tokens. Can produce very high APYs but involves compounding risks from token inflation and smart contract bugs.
04
Liquid Staking
Stake ETH or SOL and receive a liquid receipt token (stETH, mSOL) you can still use in DeFi. Earn staking rewards without locking up your assets. Lowest risk DeFi strategy.
05
Perpetual DEXes
Trade crypto with leverage on-chain. Liquidity providers earn funding fees. Highest risk — leveraged trading is dangerous. Start with spot before touching perps.
06
RWA & Stablecoin Yields
Earn yield on stablecoins backed by real-world assets (T-bills, bonds). Protocols like Ondo Finance offer 4–6% APY in USD — the DeFi equivalent of a savings account.
